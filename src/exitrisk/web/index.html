        AAppendix: service exit-risk catalog

        Every AWS service is classified into one of five tiers by how much of a workload must change to leave AWS. The tier sets the service's risk score; the recommended path and typical duration are for one workload of that service.

        BAppendix: methodology

        Data sources

        Spend comes from AWS Cost Explorer in the management (payer) account: amortised cost per account per service per month, excluding tax, credits, refunds and support. Marketplace purchases are grouped as one third-party line. Accounts and OU structure come from AWS Organizations. The optional deep scan counts resources (Lambda functions, DynamoDB tables, state machines, stacks and more) in each member account through a read-only role. All API calls are read-only.

        Scoring

        Each service's tier maps to a score: Portable 10, Low 30, Moderate 50, High 75, Critical 95. An account's inherent risk is the spend-weighted average of its services' scores.

        The report gives two OU scores. By spend weights each account by its share of in-scope spend, so a small account full of proprietary services scores high on its own yet moves the score very little. By number of accounts counts every account with spend equally, so one large account (for example a contact centre on Connect) cannot dominate it. A gap between the two shows whether risk is concentrated in a few big accounts or spread across many. An account's contribution is its inherent risk × its weight under the weighting chosen in the bar at the top (by spend unless changed).

        Ratings: Low < 25 ≤ Moderate < 40 ≤ Elevated < 55 ≤ High < 70 ≤ Critical.

        Estimate

        The cost and time figures depend on the delivery questions in section 5. Until they are answered, the report marks the figures provisional and keeps them out of the bottom line.

        Effort is built bottom-up: each material service in each account contributes the catalog's engineer-weeks for one workload, scaled by the square root of its spend relative to US$1,000/month (and by its resource count where the deep scan ran). Account overhead, a target landing zone, programme management and contingency are added, and the total is adjusted for delivery maturity from the questionnaire. Duration is the longer of effort ÷ available capacity and the critical path of the hardest tier present.

        Limitations

        • Spend is a proxy for size; a cheap service can still be hard to move (the deep scan's resource counts partly correct for this).
        • RDS spend covers all engines; Aurora-specific features and commercial engines (Oracle, SQL Server) appear in the deep scan's engine breakdown.
        • Contractual exit costs (EDP commitments, Savings Plans, Reserved Instances) and target-cloud run costs are outside the estimate.
        • The estimate is an order-of-magnitude planning figure for an executive decision, not a delivery quote.
